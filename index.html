Eye Diagram Lab

The received signal is cut into slices two symbol periods (2T) long, each centred on a sampling instant. Every slice is drawn on top of the previous ones. The paths taken by all possible bit sequences overlap into a shape that looks like an eye, and how open that eye is tells you how reliably the receiver can decide each bit.

Scenarios

Reading the eye

Eye height
Vertical opening at the sampling instant: the gap between the lowest "1" path and the highest "0" path. It is the noise margin. Smaller height means less noise is needed to cause a bit error.
Eye width
Horizontal opening along the decision threshold. It is the timing margin: how far the sampling clock may drift before errors appear.
Best sampling instant
The time where the eye is most open vertically. The receiver's clock recovery tries to sample here.
Crossing spread
Thickness of the X where paths cross the threshold, near ±T/2. It comes from timing jitter and ISI.
Slope of the sides
How fast the eye closes when sampling early or late. Steep sides mean the system is sensitive to timing error.
Rail thickness
Spread of the top and bottom paths at the sampling instant. Fuzzy rails mean noise; several distinct rails mean ISI.

What closes the eye

NoiseRails become fuzzy and the eye closes vertically. Errors start when noise bridges the gap. ISI (narrow channel)Each sample depends on neighbouring bits, so the rails split into separate lines, one per bit pattern. The eye shrinks in both directions. Timing jitterThe crossings smear sideways and the eye closes horizontally. Small roll-off αUses less bandwidth (B = (1+α)/2T) but the pulse tails ring longer. Height at the ideal instant stays full, but the eye gets narrower, so timing must be more precise. More levels (4-PAM)Twice the bits per symbol, but three stacked eyes each one third as tall. The same noise causes far more errors.

Try these

  1. Set the speed to about 1 symbol/s and press Clear eye. Watch each amber slice in the top screen land in the eye. Find a 0 1 0 pattern in the waveform and follow the path it draws.
  2. Choose Clean, set α = 1, then slowly reduce α to 0.1. The eye height at the centre stays the same. What happens to the width, and why?
  3. Choose Band-limited (ISI). Count the separate rails at the sampling instant. How many neighbouring bits does each sample depend on?
  4. Choose Noisy and raise σ until red dots appear. Now move the sampling instant to ±0.25 T. Why do errors increase faster when you sample away from the centre?
  5. Keep σ fixed and switch between 2-PAM and 4-PAM. Compare the error counts and explain the trade-off between data rate and noise margin.
